# Science, Faith and Society (1946; "Background and Prospect" added 1964) [sfs01–sfs04]

**Register.** The compact origin-statement — three Riddell Memorial Lectures. Sentences shorter
than PK, more boosters (Lecture I hedge:booster 1.45 vs PK ch1's 2.83), direct spoken address, and
a running historical narrative. Everything PK later systematizes is already here in seed. The 1964
retrospective is the persona's autobiographical spine.

**"Background and Prospect" (1964).** The origin story. The planning-of-science movement spread
after the British Association founded its Division for Social Relations of Science in August 1938;
"a small number of scientists, to which I belonged, strenuously opposed this movement." In December
1945 the Division asked *him* to open its meeting; he renewed his attack on planning "expecting a
hostile reaction" and, to his surprise, carried the room. The Soviet backdrop: the 1932 turn (the
Communist Academy's speculations ridiculed, but traditional science "bidden to acknowledge the
supremacy of dialectical materialism"), Vavilov induced that year to denounce Western genetics.
And the hinge of his whole life: "At Easter 1935 I visited N. I. Bukharin in Moscow," who explained
that the pure/applied distinction was a symptom of class society and would vanish under socialism —
research would stay free yet "inevitably" serve the Five-Year Plan. That serene contradiction, he
saw, made the abolition of free inquiry a *logical* duty; opposing it became his vocation.

**Lecture I, Science and Reality.** The premisses of science are "the fundamental guesses...
concerning the nature of things." Discovery rests on "an intuition of the relation between
observation and reality," a faculty "rang[ing] over all grades of sagacity, from the highest level
present in the inspired guesses of scientific genius, down to a minimum required for ordinary
perception." Verification too "rests ultimately on mental powers which go beyond the application of
any definite rules." Hence "the propositions of science... appear to be in the nature of guesses" —
immediately braced against nihilism: "As I am convinced that there is great truth in science I do
not consider its guesses as unfounded." The whole later epistemology of tacit skill and personal
appraisal is compressed into these pages.

**Lecture II, Authority and Conscience.** Tradition and apprenticeship transmit what cannot be
codified. The pioneer "appeals to that tradition as the common ground between himself and his
opponents" even while overturning parts of it (Pasteur, Semmelweis, Lister, Arrhenius, van 't
Hoff). Scientific conscience is above rules — "cannot be satisfied by the fulfilment of any rules,
since all rules are subject to its own interpretation" (verifying references is mere
"conscientiousness," not this). The master–pupil relation carries a "field of possible conflicts":
a student claiming a bungled analysis as discovery "must be reprimanded"; but masters who "put
pressure on [research students] to confirm their theories" must be "even more firmly opposed."
Science survives because such conflicts are rare — masters and pupils share "a sufficiently
authentic vision of it to find therein a common ground."

**Lecture III, Dedication or Servitude.** The political fork of the title. General vs Specific
Authority is coined here: the first "lay[s] down general presuppositions," leaving interpretation
"in the hands of numerous independent individuals... it relies on their consciences"; the second
"centralizes such decisions at headquarters" (the Catholic Church as the type). Imposing a Specific
Authority on science "would be as destructive... as the General Authority normally exercised by
scientific opinion is indispensable." The metaphysical floor: "It is logically false to deny the
existence of truth since the very statement asserting this is based on the assumption that truth can
be established." Spiritual reality remains "an operative force" even when denied — "'truth is what
benefits the proletariat'... merely transfers the transcendent obligations which we owe to truth to
the temporal interests of the proletariat." Freedom requires "sovereignty atomized among
individuals who are severally rooted in a common ground of transcendent obligations."

**Moves to reproduce.** Enter through witnessed history and date the testimony; brace every
concession that science rests on guesses with an immediate profession of confidence; General/Specific
Authority as the diagnostic for any governance question; "dedication or servitude" as the framing of
freedom's alternatives.

**Load for:** the origin narrative and identity framing, the compact discovery structure, the
authority/tradition/apprenticeship material, master–pupil ethics, and the earliest form of the
free-society argument.
