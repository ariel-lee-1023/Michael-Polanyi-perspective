# Personal Knowledge, Part Two: The Tacit Component (chs. 5–7) [pk05–pk07]

**Register.** Widest-ranging Part — child psychology, animal experiments (Gua the chimpanzee,
"born in captivity on November 15th, 1930, in Cuba"), Gestalt material, history of mathematics,
then the great political chapter. Exposition still phenomenology-first, but the example bank
shifts from crafts to language, passion, and community.

**Ch. 5, Articulation.** The three-point argument on man and animal: (1) man's intellectual
superiority "is almost entirely due to the use of language," yet (2) the gift of speech cannot
itself be due to language, so (3) with linguistic clues excluded, men barely beat animals at animal
problems — hence everything rests on "an almost imperceptible advantage" in inarticulate faculties.
Language operates by tacit coefficients; its powers rest on the ineffable. The tacit-articulate
spectrum has three regions: the *ineffable domain* (where articulation is impossible — knowing a
skill's particulars "only in practice, as instrumental particulars"); the domain where text and
tacit are co-extensive; the domain of sophistication (symbols outrunning understanding).
"Ineffable" is meant literally, not mystically; philosophies forbidding such talk impose "quixotic
standards" that "would reduce us all to voluntary imbecility." §13 "Dwelling In and Breaking Out":
a framework — theory, mathematics, symphony — "will be used by dwelling in it"; "astronomic
observations are made by dwelling in astronomic theory," and this internal enjoyment is what makes
the astronomer love the stars. Breaking out = crossing to a new framework: the logical structure of
discovery and of conversion alike.

**Ch. 6, Intellectual Passions.** Passions are not psychological noise; they have a *selective
function* — appraising beauty, interest, and value, deciding what is science at all — plus
heuristic and persuasive functions. "A scientific theory which calls attention to its own beauty,
and partly relies on it for claiming to represent empirical reality, is akin to a work of art."
Rival frameworks are separated by a "logical gap"; conversion across it cannot be compelled by
formal proof — persuasion works by the convert's anticipation of a richer dwelling. Mathematics
inhabited for its intellectual beauty. The chapter's stake: if science's affirmation requires
justifying passionate valuation, the justification of *every* domain of culture is opened.

**Ch. 7, Conviviality.** The social completion. Tacit sharing "underlies every single act of
articulate communication"; culture transmits by authority and apprenticeship; a society has
cultural life only insofar as it honors the obligation to support these passions — the "civic
coefficients" of intellectual life. Love of truth reappears "as the love of the kind of society
which fosters these values." Then the demonstration by negation: the Marxism section. The
unmasking sequence — dry as bone: "So far this was harmless nonsense. But soon unmasking became
more virulent" — culminating August 1948, Lysenko announcing Central Committee approval, the
Academy rising "as one man." Marxism's dual mechanism: "all science is class science" discredits
bourgeois science and accredits socialist science at once. Truth's universality replaced by
partisan righteousness = conviviality destroyed at the root. Milosz footnoted: intellectuals joined
to "have value guaranteed, if not by God, at least by history."

**Moves to reproduce.** Animal-to-man staircase arguments; the logical-gap/conversion frame for
paradigm disputes; naming the civic coefficient in any epistemic breakdown; polemic-by-chronology
(1932, 1935, 1948) with irony going dry.
