## 1. Subsidiary and focal awareness (from-to knowing)

We attend **from** subsidiary particulars **to** a focal whole. The two awarenesses are mutually
exclusive: shift focal attention onto the particulars and the integration collapses.

*Exemplar.* The hammer — subsidiary awareness of the feeling in the palm, merged into focal
awareness of driving in the nail. The pianist who attends to his own fingers gets confused and may
have to stop.

*Late restatement (Meaning, 1975).* Three aspects of the same relation: **functional** (from–to),
**phenomenal** (the new sensory quality of the stereo image, not present in the original pair), and
**semantic** — whatever a thing bears on may be called its meaning, so the focal target *is* the
meaning of the subsidiaries. An act of sense-giving.

*Diagnostic.* Where in this achievement is the unspecifiable personal participation? What is being
attended *from*?

*Depth:* `clusters/pk1-art-of-knowing.md` (ch. 4), `clusters/meaning-late.md` (chs. 2–3).

## 2. Indwelling; dwelling in and breaking out

A framework — a theory, a mathematics, a symphony, a language, a body — is used by dwelling in it.
Astronomic observations are made by dwelling in astronomic theory. Tools and probes are assimilated
to the body; we attend from them, not to them.

**Breaking out** is crossing to a new framework: the logical structure of discovery and of
conversion alike. A framework we dwell in cannot be judged as a detached object; it is judged by
dwelling in it more deeply, or by breaking out into another.

*Diagnostic.* Is the thing in front of me an object of attention or a dwelling? If a dwelling,
external criticism of it is category-mistaken.

*Depth:* `clusters/pk2-tacit-component.md` (ch. 5 §13).

## 3. The tacit–articulate spectrum

Three regions:

1. **The ineffable domain** — articulation is impossible; the particulars are known only in
   practice, as instrumental particulars.
2. **Co-extensive** — text and tacit knowledge run together.
3. **Sophistication** — symbols outrun understanding.

"Ineffable" is meant literally, not mystically. Philosophies that forbid such talk impose quixotic
standards that would reduce us to voluntary imbecility.

*Related.* **Maxims** are useful to those who already possess an art and incapable of replacing it.
**Connoisseurship** transmits only by apprenticeship, example to example.

*Depth:* `clusters/pk2-tacit-component.md` (ch. 5), `clusters/pk1-art-of-knowing.md` (ch. 4).

## 4. Personal vs subjective; universal intent

The knower's participation does not make knowledge arbitrary. The **personal** is constituted by
asserting with **universal intent**; the universal is the impersonal pole of that assertion.
**Commitment** is the frame in which the two mutually require each other. Compulsion and neurotic
obsession exclude responsibility from one side; caprice excludes it from the other.

*Exemplars.* The judge, whose discretion is narrowed to zero by the stranglehold of his own
universal intent — he must find the law, existing though as yet unknown; that is his independence.
Columbus, whose discovery was his own act, though America was there to be discovered.

*Diagnostic.* Is the chooser choosing what he *likes*, or choosing what he believes is *there*?

*Depth:* `clusters/pk3-justification.md` (ch. 10).

## 5. The fiduciary programme

Every declaratory sentence carries a tacit prefix, "I believe" — not a verb but a symbol fixing the
sentence's modality and attributing the assertion to a definite person at a particular place and
time.

The objection ("then you can believe what you like") is answered by acceptance, not retreat: so be
it; only this manner of holding beliefs is consonant with itself. The alternative — knowledge
guaranteed by no one, asserted by no one, held at no one's risk — is not stricter, it is empty.

*Diagnostic (prefix analysis).* Who is affirming this, at what risk, and would the affirmation
survive being applied to itself?

*Depth:* `clusters/pk3-justification.md` (chs. 8–9).

## 6. Symmetry-then-dedication

The move for any relativism or social-construction challenge. **First concede fully:** every belief
system is stabilized by circularity, a self-expanding interpretive idiom, reserves of secondary
elaboration, and suppressed nucleation of rival concepts. The Azande poison-oracle and modern
naturalism share this logical structure exactly; the concession cannot be made too generously.

**Then locate the difference,** which is never a demarcation formula: it is the *direction of
dedication* — a community pledged to reality, submitting its guesses to standards held with
universal intent, trained in a tradition that honours the pioneer who appeals to that tradition
against its current interpreters.

*Depth:* `clusters/pk3-justification.md` (ch. 9), `clusters/sfs-lectures.md` (Lecture II).

## 7. Intellectual passions

Passions are not psychological noise. Three functions:

- **Selective** — appraising beauty, interest, and value; deciding what counts as science at all.
- **Heuristic** — a problem is the scent of a hidden reality; to see a good problem is half the
  discovery.
- **Persuasive** — rival frameworks are separated by a *logical gap*; conversion across it cannot be
  compelled by formal proof, and works instead through the convert's anticipation of a richer
  dwelling.

A theory that calls attention to its own beauty, and partly relies on it in claiming to represent
empirical reality, is akin to a work of art.

*Depth:* `clusters/pk2-tacit-component.md` (ch. 6).

## 8. Corporate vs spontaneous order; polycentricity

- **Corporate order** — persons assigned to a pattern by command through a hierarchy. Limited by
  **span of control**: for a delicate and rapidly changing task, usually not more than three to five
  subordinates, because the significant relations among subordinates rise steeply with their number.
- **Spontaneous order** — mutual adjustment of independent initiatives, each attending to the
  published results of all the others, coordinated by a shared task. The **jigsaw-puzzle model**:
  many hands on one puzzle, each seeing every other's moves, on the twofold condition that each
  step can be readily judged for correctness and is rapidly brought to everyone's notice.
- **Polycentric task** — one with many interdependent centres. Handed to a central directorate it
  is not done badly; it is not done at all, though something else may be done in its name and under
  its name. Central direction reduces joint effectiveness to that of the single person directing
  from the centre.

Markets solve their polycentric task through the price and profit signal — which is why the signal
must be protected (the patent problem: an inventor cannot recover invested capital once the
invention is free to all). Economic liberty is a **social technique**, not a metaphysical absolute.

*Diagnostic.* Before judging an institution, ask which kind of order its task demands.

*Depth:* `clusters/lol-essays.md` (chs. 4–5, 8–10).

## 9. General vs Specific Authority

- **General Authority** lays down general presuppositions and leaves their interpretation to
  numerous independent individuals, relying on their consciences.
- **Specific Authority** centralizes decisions at headquarters.

Science lives under the first and dies under the second. Imposing a Specific Authority on science
would be as destructive as the General Authority of scientific opinion is indispensable. The same
holds for law, for scholarship, for any tradition that means to grow.

*Corollary (academic freedom).* A government can honour academic freedom only where scientific
opinion has on the whole agreed; where opinion is sharply divided, the freedom cannot be maintained
— the General Authority must exist in order to be respected. The **judge analogy**: that the King
appoints and pays the judges does not affect their independence so long as the King is under the
law; patronage becomes an undertaking to provide fuel and oil for a machine whose operation the
provider does not control.

*Depth:* `clusters/sfs-lectures.md` (Lecture III), `clusters/lol-essays.md` (ch. 3).

## 10. Operational principles and levels

Machines and organisms are defined by **operational principles**, not by physics and chemistry. An
operational principle is a *rule of rightness*: it says how the thing works *when it works*, and so
admits categories physics does not possess — success and failure, function and malfunction. It
functions as an ideal, the ideal of the machine in good working order, as geometrical
crystallography ignores the imperfections of crystals. A complete physical-chemical description of
a clock would never yield the concept "clock".

*Diagnostic (the reduction test).* Convert "is X reducible to Y?" into "does Y possess the
categories under which X is even described?"

*Depth:* `clusters/pk4-knowing-being.md` (chs. 11–13).

## 11. Natural vs transnatural integration

- **Natural integration** — mathematics and physics join incompatibles that come nevertheless to
  seem naturally compatible.
- **Transnatural integration** — art and myth join incompatibles that *remain* incompatible. These
  carry us away into their own sphere, away from the sphere in which we lived a moment ago and to
  which we shall presently return.

*Related.* A work of art is the mutual embodiment of a **frame** and a **story**; poetic
artificialities (metre, rhyme, expressive sounds, strange connotations, above all metaphor) function
as subsidiaries which, combined with the content that could be put into prose, form the poem's
meaning. Sacred acts are essentially detached affirmations, isolated by ritual from the flow of time
and the surroundings of space.

*Depth:* `clusters/meaning-late.md` (chs. 4–10).

## 12. Moral inversion

Scepticism shatters the traditional forms for holding moral ideals; the moral passions are then
diverted into the only channels a strictly mechanistic conception of man and society leaves open.
The morally inverted person acts with the whole force of his homeless moral passions inside a purely
materialistic framework of purposes. The fanatic is a disappointed sceptic.

*Genealogy.* Bentham and the sceptical English are protected by inconsistency — they live by
traditional beliefs their philosophy denies. Those who take such philosophies seriously and try to
live by their light (Bazarov; the heirs of Nietzsche and Stirner; the German Youth Movement of
1910–30) pass from private nihilism to the political nihilism of scientific utopianism.

*Related.* Spiritual reality remains an operative force even when denied. It is logically false to
deny the existence of truth, since the statement asserting the denial claims to be true; and "truth
is what benefits the proletariat" does not cancel the love of truth, it transfers truth's
transcendent claims to a temporal power — which is worse.

`clusters/sfs-lectures.md` (Lecture III).
