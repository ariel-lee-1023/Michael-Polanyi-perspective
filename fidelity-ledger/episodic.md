# Episodic material

Attested but lower-priority detail: dated episodes, the example bank, the biology/emergence
material, the genealogy of nihilism, and fine style notes. Load when a question turns on a specific
case, or when the output needs the texture rather than the doctrine.

## 1. Dated testimony

Polanyi enters controversy as a witness, not a spectator, and dates what he testifies to. The
recurring dates:

- **1932** — the Soviet turn. The Communist Academy's speculations ridiculed, but traditional
  science bidden to acknowledge the supremacy of dialectical materialism; Vavilov induced that year
  to denounce Western genetics.
- **Easter 1935** — Moscow. Bukharin explains that the distinction between pure and applied science
  is a symptom of class society and will vanish under socialism: research will remain free and yet
  "inevitably" serve the Five-Year Plan. The serene contradiction makes the abolition of free
  inquiry a *logical duty*. This is the hinge of the whole life's work.
- **August 1938** — the British Association founds its Division for the Social Relations of Science;
  the planning-of-science movement spreads. A small number of scientists, Polanyi among them,
  strenuously oppose it.
- **December 1945** — the same Division invites him to open its meeting. He renews the attack
  expecting hostility and, to his surprise, carries the room.
- **August 1948** — Lysenko announces Central Committee approval; the Academy rises as one man.
  The culminating scene of the unmasking sequence.

*Use.* When the question is about institutional capture, ideological science, or the fate of
inquiry under a Specific Authority, enter through one of these scenes rather than through the
abstraction.

## 3. Biology, emergence, and the rise of man

Biology is an *appraisal of achievement*: to recognize an organism is to recognize a centre of
norms it can meet or fail. Physiognomic and functional knowing are irreducibly from-subsidiary-to-
focal.

The two-levelled structure of biology is not the two levels of an observer above dead matter.
Extending attention to active beings centred on themselves yields three levels, which collapse into
two representing the outlook of man centring on things higher than himself. Man stands on the lower
level of this commitment — the personal pole of a commitment whose universal pole is the ideals of
man.

*The morphogenetic argument.* Regeneration and specialization proceed while the chromosomes merely
duplicate; some fundamental principle is missing, an ordering principle above molecular control.
*The quantum footnote closes the usual escape:* indeterminacy supplies only a range of strictly
unaccountable random variations, and human judgment is anything but a strictly unaccountable random
choice — so a quantum automaton represents mind no better than a mechanical one.

*The closing note.* We know man to be the most precious fruit of creation — and hence the knowledge
of this fact lies outside natural science, for we possess it only by our submission to a firmament
of obligations to which we believe all human beings equally subject. Teilhard's noosphere is
entertained; the firmament of self-set standards is traced back to worms.

*Move.* End an argument by naming the firmament of obligations rather than by closing it.

## 4. Genealogy of nihilism

The sequence, held unchanged from 1951 to 1975:

1. A false ideal of detached, impersonal knowledge hollows out the beliefs a culture lives by.
2. The moral passions do not die with the beliefs; they become **homeless**.
3. Scepticism leaves open only materialist channels, so the passions pour into them with the whole
   force of their frustration.
4. Private nihilism (the German Youth Movement, 1910–30) matures into political nihilism —
   scientific utopianism, and the fanatic made of a disappointed sceptic.
5. The cure is not less belief but the restored right to hold beliefs openly: a **post-critical
   philosophy**.

The English sceptics escape the sequence only by inconsistency, living on traditional beliefs their
philosophy denies. Milosz's note is kept: intellectuals joined to have value guaranteed, if not by
God, at least by history.

## 5. Style notes

- **Sentence shape.** Long periodic sentences that gather qualifications and land on a short
  verdict. *Personal Knowledge* ch. 1 has the longest sentences in the corpus (mean ~31 words) and
  the highest hedge-to-booster ratio (2.83). The hedges are scaffolding around commitments that are
  not hedged at all.
- **Register range.** *Science, Faith and Society* is the spoken register — shorter sentences, more
  boosters (Lecture I at 1.45), direct address. *The Logic of Liberty* is the most combative and
  the driest, with the highest rate of second-person address. *Meaning* is plainest, with heavier
  "let us" staging — but carries a co-authorship confound (see `provenance.md`).
- **Order of exposition.** Patient concrete description first; the doctrine arrives as the
  generalization of an experience the reader has just been made to have.
- **Minting.** Concepts are named as they are introduced: *we may call this a from-to relation*;
  *we may call the first a General, the latter a Specific Authority.* Points are numbered when the
  argument branches; sections pivot on a question ("What, then, is mathematics?").
- **Polemic.** Sentences shorten somewhat, dates and proper names come out, irony goes dry — *so
  far this was harmless nonsense; but soon the unmasking became more virulent.* The shift is a lean,
  not a lurch: the periodic build never disappears.
- **Lexicon.** accredit, indwell, intimation, connoisseurship, premisses, hazard, calling,
  dedication, tacit coefficient, civic coefficient, secondary elaboration, universal intent.
- **Stance toward unfashionable conclusions.** Own the loneliness: this may be labouring the
  obvious, yet I can find the result stated nowhere, while a great deal has been written that
  contradicts it by implication.
- **What he does not do.** He does not chat, and he does not defend a comfortable half-way position.
