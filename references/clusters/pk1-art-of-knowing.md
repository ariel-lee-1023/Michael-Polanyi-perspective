# Personal Knowledge, Part One: The Art of Knowing (chs. 1–4) [pk01–pk04]

**Register.** The book's slowest, most architectonic prose — ch. 1 has the corpus's longest
sentences (mean ~31 words) and its highest hedge:booster ratio (2.83). The hedges are scaffolding;
the commitments underneath are absolute. Method throughout: begin from an exact, concrete case the
reader can verify, then extract the structure.

**Ch. 1, Objectivity.** Opens by asking what the Copernican revolution actually teaches. The answer
inverts the textbook lesson: Copernicus abandoned the terrestrial station for the greater
"intellectual satisfaction" of the solar panorama, so the new system was "as anthropocentric as the
Ptolemaic view" — it merely satisfied a different human affection, the delight in abstract theory.
Objectivity is then *rebuilt* on this admission: a theory is more objective than raw sense because
it is "something other than myself," a map that "cannot be deluded and remains right or wrong in
itself" — I rely on it, and it, not I, is proved right or wrong. Two famous reductios of the
detached ideal: the faithful film of cosmic history in which all human existence "would flash by in
a single second," and the equal-attention-to-equal-mass survey yielding "a lifelong preoccupation
with interstellar dust." Verdict sentence of the whole book: eliminating the human perspective
"must lead to absurdity." A true theory's mark is *prophetic power* — relativity confirmed by
effects unknown to Einstein.

**Ch. 2, Probability.** Statistics is chosen deliberately as the hardest case: the most formalized
corner of science is shown to rest on a *personal act of appraisal*. Accepting that three double
sixes run one in 46,656, "I shall entertain a correspondingly small expectation" — the number
guides and expresses *my* participation. Probability statements "make no predictions which could be
contradicted by any conceivable events"; only a personal appraisal can hold them to experience. He
walks through Fisher's analysis of Darwin's plant data (standard deviation computed in full) to
show even significance-testing terminates in appraisal. Also here: the fiduciary mode is
foreshadowed — universal validity ascribed to appraisals.

**Ch. 3, Order.** Randomness and significant pattern are correlative: probability statements apply
to random systems, and randomness itself is "the ultimate, not further analysable condition" of the
calculus (Campbell cited approvingly). Significant order = the operation of an ordering principle;
hence "the assumption of an accidental formation of the living species is a logical muddle... a
piece of equivocation." This chapter quietly plants the anti-reductionist charge that Part Four
detonates.

**Ch. 4, Skills.** The load-bearing chapter of the whole system. The hammer: "I have a subsidiary
awareness of the feeling in the palm of my hand which is merged into my focal awareness of my
driving in the nail." The two awarenesses are *mutually exclusive* — the pianist who attends to his
fingers "gets confused and may have to stop." Skillful performance follows rules "not known as such
to the person following them" (the cyclist's balancing formula; the swimmer's buoyancy). Maxims:
useful to those who already possess the art, incapable of replacing it. Connoisseurship transmitted
only by apprenticeship, "example to example." Tools and probes are *assimilated to the body* — we
attend from them; externality itself is defined by subsidiary awareness of bodily processes. The
"unspecifiability" of particulars introduced here becomes the ineffable domain of ch. 5.

**Moves to reproduce from this Part.** The inversion-of-the-official-lesson opening; rebuilding a
concept (objectivity) on the ground just cleared beneath it; choosing the opponent's strongest
territory (statistics) for the demonstration; numbered (a)(b)(c) analyses; the section-pivot
question ("What, then, is mathematics?").

**Load for:** objectivity/anthropocentrism questions, probability and formalization limits, design
vs randomness arguments, skill/expertise/tacit-craft questions, the hammer-pianist-cyclist example
bank.
